Is pre-IPO investing right for you?

Before a company goes public, early investors sometimes get access — but it isn't for everyone. Here's a clear-eyed look at the upside, the risks, and who this kind of investing actually suits, before you consider it.

Ask a question See the risks first Common questions
The opportunity

Getting in before the public market does

Pre-IPO investing means buying a stake in a private company before it lists on a public exchange — often through a private placement, a fund, or a secondary sale of existing shares.

  • Potential access to growth that may already be mostly priced in by the time a company reaches the public market
  • A way to diversify beyond publicly traded stocks and bonds
  • Exposure to companies at an earlier, potentially higher-growth stage
The risks

What most people don't hear about

The upside gets most of the attention. The risks are just as real, and worth understanding before anything else.

  • IlliquidShares typically can't be sold on the open market — your money may be tied up for years with no guaranteed exit
  • Limited dataPrivate companies disclose far less than public ones, so due diligence is harder and information gaps are common
  • Higher minimumsInvestments are often structured for accredited or qualified investors, with investment minimums to match
  • No guaranteeNot every private company goes public, or succeeds — some investments return nothing
Who this suits

Is this the right fit for your portfolio?

Pre-IPO investing tends to make the most sense for investors who meet a specific profile — not as a replacement for a core portfolio, but as a smaller, considered allocation alongside one.

  • Accredited or qualified investors, as defined by SEC rules
  • A longer time horizon, since an exit isn't guaranteed on any set schedule
  • Comfort allocating a smaller slice of a diversified portfolio to a higher-risk, illiquid position
